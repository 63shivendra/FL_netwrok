Main objective

Privacy-preserving federated learning for hospitals

Hospitals want better disease-prediction models, but privacy laws such as HIPAA and GDPR stop them from pooling patient records. This project shows they can train together without sharing any raw data. Each hospital trains on its own patients, and only model outputs travel to a central server. This approach is called federated learning, a form of distributed machine learning.

PrivacyPatient rows never leave the hospital. Only predicted probabilities are shared.
DistributedEach hospital trains locally, and each can use its own model family (a neural network and an SVM).
CollaborativeThe server averages the hospitals' predictions and sends the consensus back, so each model learns from the other.

From FHIR bundles to tensors

Data pipeline

Synthetic patient records (Synthea FHIR JSON) are flattened, filtered, imputed and standardized. The result is a single table of 44 numeric features plus a three-class prognosis label.

  1. Parse FHIR bundlesdataprocees.pyWalks every patient JSON in chunks of 500. Pulls demographics, conditions, medications, and encounter, observation and procedure counts.
  2. Flatten to a wide tablefiltered_dataset_condition3500updated.csv129,218 patients with one-hot allergies, lab observations and 28 condition flags.
  3. Impute missing labsdatahandlingmissingvalues.py · fianlcleanhandling.ipynbComputes age from birth date. Fills missing labs with the median for the patient's age group (infant, child, adult, elderly).
  4. Final modelling tablefinaldataset.csv rows × 46 columns, 44 numeric features, missing values.
  5. Stratified federated splitserver.py · load_and_split_data60% train (split between two hospitals), 20% shared reference set, 20% held-out test. StandardScaler is fit on training rows only.

Runs live in your browser

Federation simulator

This trains real models in your browser on a stratified sample of the actual dataset: 3,000 training patients and 1,000 test patients, standardized, with IDs removed. Change the number of hospitals, how unevenly their patients are distributed, and how much noise is added to what they share. The simulator then compares the project's probability averaging with FedAvg, hospitals training alone, and pooling all the data.

Label skew drawn from a Dirichlet distribution. At high skew, one hospital might see almost no class 0 patients.
Gaussian noise added to whatever leaves a hospital (weight updates or probabilities). This illustrates the accuracy cost of differential privacy. It is not a formal ε guarantee.
